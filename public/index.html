1. Provenance & Clinical Hardware Specifications

The dataset was acquired and curated by Julio César Mello Román (Kaggle: Orvile) and co-authors at the Departamento de Radiología, Facultad de Odontología, Universidad Nacional de Asunción in Asunción, Paraguay. It was formally published in Sensors (MDPI, 2021).

  • Imaging System: Owandy I-max Touch high-frequency panoramic digital radiography equipment.
  • Sensor Characteristics: CCD sensor with optical fiber coupling, producing standardized 2:1 aspect ratio panoramic orthopantomograms.
  • Format & Dynamic Range: Uncompressed 8-bit grayscale standard format (dimensions 2041 × 1024 pixels).
  • Cohort Diversity: Encompasses mixed and permanent dentitions, edentulous spaces, impacted third molars, metallic amalgam and composite resin restorations, endodontic fillings, and varying degrees of horizontal/vertical bone resorption.

2. Detailed Diagnostic Criteria for the 3 Diseases

Dental Caries

Hard Tissue Demineralization

Characterized by localized radiolucencies in coronal enamel, dentin, or root surfaces where acid-producing bacteria have demineralized hydroxyapatite crystals.

  • Coronal Caries: Radiolucent notch on occlusal or proximal surfaces extending toward the pulp chamber.
  • Interproximal Caries: Triangle-shaped radiolucency with its base at the outer enamel surface, located immediately apical to the interproximal contact area.
  • Recurrent (Secondary) Caries: Radiolucent halo or crescent beneath existing restorations.
  • Differential Diagnosis: Cervical burnout, radiographic mach band effect, composite restoration radiolucencies.

Periapical Lesion

Endodontic Apical Periodontitis

Pathology originating from pulpal necrosis that spreads through the apical foramen into the surrounding periapical bone architecture.

  • Early Stage: Widening of the periodontal ligament (PDL) space (> 0.5 mm) at the anatomical root apex.
  • Moderate Stage: Loss of continuous lamina dura with well-defined or diffuse periapical radiolucency (< 5 mm).
  • Advanced Cyst/Granuloma: Large (> 5 mm) corticated radiolucent lesion, potentially displacing adjacent cortical plates.
  • Differential Diagnosis: Mental foramen, incisive canal, maxillary sinus extension, apical cemental dysplasia (early stage).

Periodontitis

Alveolar Bone Resorption

Chronic inflammatory loss of supporting alveolar bone crest around the root structures of permanent teeth.

  • Normal Baseline: Alveolar bone crest is positioned 1.0 to 1.5 mm apical to the cementoenamel junction (CEJ) with a dense cortical margin.
  • Horizontal Bone Loss: Bilateral uniform reduction in alveolar bone height exceeding 2.0 mm apical to the CEJ across multiple teeth.
  • Vertical (Angular) Defects: Oblique bone resorption progressing along the root surface.
  • Furcation Involvement: Radiolucency in the inter-radicular space between roots of bi- or tri-furcated molars.

3. Scientific Image Enhancement Pipeline (Román et al.)

The original paper (*Sensors* 2021) introduced morphological enhancement techniques to overcome radiographic noise, cervical burnout, and low contrast inherent in panoramic dental equipment:

  • Multi-Scale Top-Hat transform with Geodesic Reconstruction (MSTHGR): Isolates fine anatomical details and edge transitions while preserving structural connectivity.
  • Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE): Applied locally across 8×8 pixel tiles to equalize dynamic range without amplifying background photon noise, making faint periapical lesions visible.
  • Pre-processing Standardization: Resampling to standardized 1024 × 512 dimensions preserves the exact 2:1 panoramic aspect ratio, ensuring no geometric distortion of the dental arch or tooth anatomy.
